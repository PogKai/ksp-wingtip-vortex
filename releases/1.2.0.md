# WingtipVortex 1.2.0 — Wing Vapor

1.1.0 taught the mod rockets. **1.2.0 adds wing vapor** — the white sheet that forms over a fighter's
wings in a hard pull — and gives the wingtip vortices three pieces of real physics: their cores spread
the way a real wake's do, the ground pushes them apart, and a strongly loaded core can burst.

## Highlights

### Wing vapor

Real wing vapor isn't an effect somebody switched on. Air flowing over the top of a wing drops in
pressure, cools, and where it cools past its dew point the water in it condenses. The mod does that
calculation for every lifting part, every physics step: how hard the part is working, how that load is
spread along the chord, how much the air cools, how much water condenses out.

* A thin haze along the leading edge first, spreading back over the wing as the pull tightens, thinner
  toward the tips, different on each wing in a bank
* **It's hard to get.** A passenger jet at its structural limit shows a faint trace at most, a fighter
  needs a real pull in humid air, and a takeoff roll, cruise or a slow approach shows nothing
* Slow, hard-working wings condense sooner than fast ones at the same load, and wings near Mach 1
  sooner still — as real ones do
* Wings built from many parts, clipped or stacked, count as the one wing they form; control surfaces
  are part of their section; parts buried in a fuselage or nacelle make no vapor
* **Lower atmosphere only:** a hard cutoff at 10% of the body's sea-level density (about 14 km on
  Kerbin). Above it there is no vapor and the code does no work, so orbit and space stations cost
  nothing
* White in sunlight, dimmed on the night side from the star's actual flux
* No extra assets: KSP's own particle shader and a texture generated in code

### Physics for the vortices

* **Viscous core growth.** Lamb-Oseen spreading fed by Squire's eddy viscosity, so a heavy aircraft's
  wake holds together as a tight rope while a light one goes soft in seconds. Widening is paired with
  dimming, so a spread core is fainter rather than a glowing cone
* **Ground effect.** A mirror vortex under the ground cancels the pair's descent and pushes each
  vortex outboard: behind a low pass the two ropes splay apart instead of running parallel
* **Vortex breakdown, spiral and bubble.** A core with too much swirl for its axial flow kinks into a
  precessing corkscrew or bursts into a bubble, closer to the tip the harder the load

### Also

* When Ferram Aerospace Research is running, the load factor is read from FAR's own aerodynamic force
  (FARAPI, by reflection — no dependency)
* Line mode reuses one gradient instead of allocating per source per frame

## Install

Download `WingtipVortex_1.2.0.zip` and drop the `WingtipVortex` folder from it into `GameData/`. To
upgrade, replace the old `WingtipVortex` folder. No dependencies, no configuration, no GUI.

## Compatibility

* KSP 1.12.x, stock aerodynamics (`ModuleLiftingSurface` / `ModuleControlSurface`). Developed and
  tested on 1.12.5, including a heavily modded install (B9 Aerospace, BDArmory, TUFX and others)
* Body-relative: nothing is keyed to altitude in metres, so it is intended to behave the same on any
  atmosphere (only Kerbin has been flown)
* **Not compatible with Ferram Aerospace Research.** Vortex placement and the wing vapor detect the
  stock lifting modules FAR removes

## Known limitations

* KSP has no weather, so the air's moisture for wing vapor is a stand-in: a fixed dew-point spread
  (12 K in the humid lowest layer, 18 K above it)
* A surface lifting against the aircraft's net lift (a tailplane trimming the aircraft, a canard set
  against the wing) makes no wing vapor. KSP loads these far harder than a real aircraft would, and
  they looked like artifacts. It's a style choice, not physics
* Wing vapor is drawn on the active vessel only, as a particle effect: not volumetric, no shadows
* The breakdown thresholds are literature values applied to an estimated swirl; the mod logs what real
  flights reach so they can be tuned

## Performance

On a 137-part fighter in hard turns, the wing vapor's physics averaged under 2.5 ms per physics step
and drawing it about a quarter of a millisecond a frame. Larger craft are updated every 2 to 4 physics
steps, invisibly. There is a fixed cap on particles.

## Reporting a problem

Search `KSP.log` for `[VORTEX]`. For the detailed wing-vapor log, create an empty file named
`verbose.txt` in the `WingtipVortex` folder (next to `Plugins`), fly, and include the
`[VORTEX] wing vapor:` lines. The vortex effects log a one-shot line when each first engages, and a
`[VORTEX] session peaks` summary when you leave the flight.
